Signs it is worth checking

You do not need to see mold to have a problem. If you notice more than one of these, testing is a good idea.

  • A musty smell that will not go away.
  • Allergy or breathing symptoms that are worse indoors.
  • Water stains or discoloration on walls or ceilings.
  • Peeling paint or warped materials.
  • A recent leak or water damage.

What an inspection includes

  • Indoor air samples from the affected areas, compared against an outdoor baseline.
  • Spore count analysis by a certified lab.
  • Surface sampling where growth is visible.
  • Thermal imaging to find moisture hidden behind walls and ceilings.
  • Written findings and a clear recommendation.

Why not a hardware store test kit? A DIY kit cannot tell you where the mold is, how much there is, or what is causing it. A professional inspection answers all three, which is what you need to decide what to do next.

Common questions

Do I need to see mold to get tested?

No. Musty odors, symptoms that are worse indoors, and recent water damage are all reasons to test, even when nothing is visible.

How does air testing work?

We take air samples inside the affected areas and compare them with an outdoor baseline. Elevated spore counts confirm airborne contamination, even when you cannot see any growth.

What happens if you find mold?

You get written findings and a clear recommendation. If remediation is needed, we explain the scope and give you an itemized breakdown before any work begins.

Can you test a home I am buying or selling?

Yes. We work with buyers, sellers, and realtors on tight closing timelines and provide the documentation lenders and buyers ask for.
