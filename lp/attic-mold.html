Why Maine attics grow mold

In winter, warm, humid air from the living space rises into the attic through gaps around wiring, plumbing, light fixtures, and the attic hatch. When that air reaches the cold roof sheathing, it condenses. Maine winters keep those surfaces cold for months, so the moisture keeps coming back.

It gets worse when:

  • Bathroom or dryer vents end in the attic instead of outside.
  • The attic hatch or a chimney is not insulated.
  • There is not enough insulation or air sealing.
  • Ventilation cannot carry the moist air out.

Signs to look for

  • A musty smell, especially near the top floor.
  • Water stains on the ceiling below the attic.
  • Dark spots on the underside of the roof sheathing.

Common questions

Is attic mold dangerous?

Mold affects people differently. It can make allergy and asthma symptoms worse and lower your indoor air quality, and the moisture behind it can cause structural damage over time if it continues.

Will more insulation alone fix it?

Usually not on its own. The moisture is carried by air leaking up from the house, so air sealing and ventilation matter as much as insulation. We look at all three.
