Water in Your Home Right Now?

What to Do in the First Hour of Water Damage

Water damage gets worse by the hour. It wicks up drywall, soaks into insulation and subfloors, and runs along framing to places you cannot see. What you do before help arrives makes a real difference to how much of your home can be saved.

Do these first

  1. Stop the water. Shut off the main water valve, or the supply valve to the fixture or appliance that failed.
  2. Make it safe. If water is near outlets, cords, or the electrical panel, turn off power to that area at the breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Photograph everything before anything is moved or thrown away. Your insurance adjuster will ask for it.
  4. Move what you can (rugs, electronics, furniture, boxes) out of the wet area and up off the floor.

Our Process

How Mattra Restores a Water-Damaged Home

Every job follows the same order, because skipping a step is how homes end up with mold behind brand-new drywall.

1

Stop the Source

Find where the water is coming from and make sure it has stopped, whether that is a supply line, a roof leak, or groundwater coming through the foundation.

2

Map the Moisture

Water travels. We trace how far it went, behind walls, under flooring, and into ceilings below, so the drying plan covers the whole affected area, not just the puddle you can see.

3

Remove the Water and What Cannot Be Saved

Extract standing water, then remove saturated materials that will not dry well, like carpet pad, soaked insulation, and the bottom of wet drywall. Everything that can be saved stays.

4

Dry the Structure, Then Prove It

Drying equipment runs until moisture readings show the framing and subfloor are back to normal. Closing up a wall that only feels dry is how hidden mold starts.

5

Check for Mold

Before anything is rebuilt, we inspect the opened areas for mold growth. If it is there, it gets removed properly first.

6

Repair and Rebuild

Insulation, drywall, ceilings, flooring, and trim go back in, finished to match the rest of the home.

7

Fix What Caused It

If the cause was a wet basement, an ice dam, or poor drainage, we show you how to keep it from happening again, from waterproofing and dehumidification to air sealing and attic ventilation.

Why It Cannot Wait

Water Damage Gets Worse Every Day It Sits

The EPA recommends drying wet building materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. After that, drywall loses strength, wood swells and stains, insulation stops working, and mold takes hold. The type of water matters too, because it decides what can be dried and what has to go.

Clean Water

Category 1

From a supply line, a burst pipe, or a faucet. Most materials can be dried in place if work starts quickly. Left sitting, clean water does not stay clean.

Gray Water

Category 2

From a washing machine, dishwasher, or sump pump overflow. It carries contaminants, so porous materials like carpet pad usually come out.

Black Water

Category 3

Sewage backups and outside floodwater. Porous materials it touched have to be removed, and the area needs cleaning and sanitizing before it is rebuilt.

Water Damage in Maine Homes

The Water Damage We See Most in Lewiston-Auburn

Maine homes get water damage in ways warmer places do not. After decades of working in Central Maine homes, these are the jobs that come up again and again:

  • Frozen and burst pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces, and unheated rooms during a cold snap
  • Ice dams pushing meltwater under the shingles and into ceilings and walls
  • Spring snowmelt and heavy rain flooding basements, especially older fieldstone and block foundations
  • Sump pump failures during storms and power outages
  • Slow plumbing leaks that rot subfloors and feed mold for months before anyone notices

Many of these are not just plumbing problems. Ice dams come from heat escaping into the attic, and wet basements come from drainage and foundation issues. Because Mattra also works on air sealing, attic ventilation, and basement waterproofing, we can fix the cause, not just the damage.
