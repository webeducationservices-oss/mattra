01

The short version

if you read nothing else
Three things drive every recommendation in this dossier.

1. Google does not know Mattra does construction. Over the last 12 months the site earned 19,667 search impressions for insulation and 16,003 for mold. For remodeling and construction it earned 39. For roofing, 145. For siding, zero. The construction side of the business is effectively invisible, so remodels cannot come from organic search this year. Paid is the only near-term lever.

2. The remodel money is in the word "contractor." bathroom remodel costs $46.21 a click at competition index 33. bathroom remodel contractors costs $14.83 at competition index 8, for 320 searches a month, and it is growing 114% year over year. The hiring-intent variants are cheaper, higher intent, and almost unclaimed in Maine.

3. Ice dams are the biggest mispriced opportunity in the state, and they are a lottery. Combined ice dam and roof raking searches hit 6,800 in February 2025 and 3,200 in February 2026, at a top-of-page bid of $0.24. But February 2024 was 210. This is weather-triggered, not calendar-triggered, and it needs a standing campaign that can scale in 48 hours rather than a scheduled February budget.

The three plays

ranked by return per dollar

1. Own ice dam season, but as a standing trap rather than a booked campaign

Highest intent, lowest competition, and it maps straight into the core business

Maine ice dam and roof raking volume by month, actual searches:

February2023202420252026
ice dam390702,4001,300
roof raking5901404,4001,900

Two of the last four winters produced a genuine surge and two produced almost nothing. Google Trends confirms the mechanism: over five years, ice dam interest in the climate-matched Boston market was essentially flat except for a single five-week run from late January 2026, when it went from nothing to peak and back.

Why this is a Mattra job and not a snow-removal job. Ice dams are caused by heat escaping into the attic and melting snow that refreezes at the eaves. The people advertising against these terms sell roof raking, which removes the symptom. Mattra sells the diagnosis: attic insulation, air sealing and ventilation. An ice dam lead in February is an insulation job with a deadline and a homeowner who has just watched water come through a ceiling.

  • Build the campaign and the landing page now, in September, while it is cheap and quiet.
  • Run it year round at a floor budget of a few dollars a day so quality score and history accrue.
  • Set a rule to raise the cap hard within 48 hours of the first freeze-thaw event, not on a date.
  • Landing page has to answer the panic question first, then sell the permanent fix.

The generic research terms are expensive, contested and shrinking. The hiring terms are cheap, unclaimed and growing. A $0.00 top-of-page bid means Google has no meaningful auction data because nobody in Maine is bidding.

Timing splits in two, and it is worth exploiting. Generic bathroom remodel peaks in February, when people are indoors planning. But bathroom remodel contractors peaks in June, when they are ready to hire. Put content and remarketing against the February planners, and put the ad budget against the June hirers.

The blocker is the site, not the budget. Mattra has no kitchen, bathroom, basement finishing or addition page. /construction mentions remodels only as something that uncovers rot. Sending "bathroom remodel contractors" traffic to a page about moisture diagnosis will convert badly no matter how cheap the click is. Build the pages before buying the clicks.

3. Take the summer basement moisture season seriously

Highest-volume seasonal swing on the board, and Mattra already sells it

Dehumidification and air quality swings 8.5x across the year, peaking hard from late June through August. dehumidifier basement alone is 1,000 searches a month in Maine at a $4.40 top bid. Mattra already lists whole-home dehumidification as a service, and the Maine summer basement is the exact conversation that leads to crawl space encapsulation, vapor barriers and mold work.

This year that window has closed. It reopens around June 10 and runs to mid August, which is also when mold searches climb. Treat them as one campaign, since they are the same customer with the same damp basement.

05

Peaks and valleys

what to do in each

In a peak

  • Raise budget caps before the peak starts, not during it. Google's learning phase costs days you do not have in a five-week window.
  • Bid on the panic term and sell the permanent fix on the page.
  • Answer the phone. Paid converts by phone as often as by form at Mattra, and peak callers do not leave voicemails.
  • Do not add new keywords mid-peak. Test in the valley so the peak runs on proven copy.

In a valley

  • Build the next peak's landing pages and ad copy. September is the right month to build the February ice dam campaign.
  • Publish content against terms that peak later. Organic takes months, so write about ice dams in autumn.
  • Keep a floor budget running so account history and quality score survive.
  • Chase reviews. 61 reviews at 4.9 is good and cheap to improve when the crews are less busy.
A structural warning about Maine. Nine of the eighteen service lines here peak between December and March, when field work is hardest and daylight is shortest. The demand calendar and the delivery calendar are close to inverted. The lines that peak in winter are mostly diagnostic and interior work, which is exactly what can be sold in February and delivered through the spring. Selling a February ice dam customer an April attic job is the model that fits this state.

Method and limits

so the numbers can be checked
  • Volume and seasonality: Google Ads Keyword Planner, geo-targeted to Maine (geoTargetConstants/21154), 47 months from September 2022 to July 2026, 154 keywords. Seasonal indices are a ratio-to-moving-average decomposition: a 12-month centred moving average estimates trend, each month is divided by it, and the median ratio per calendar month becomes the index. That separates season from trend, which a single year of data cannot do.
  • Keyword Planner volumes are bucketed by Google (values land on 70, 90, 110, 140, 170, 210, 260, 320). Treat them as ranges, not counts. Ratios between keywords are reliable; precise absolute volumes are not.
  • Google Trends supplied sub-monthly timing over five years of weekly data. Maine-level Trends is too sparse for niche terms, returning zero for most weeks, so the shape checks used Massachusetts as a climate-matched, denser proxy. Where this dossier cites Trends it is for timing, never for volume.
  • Mattra's own position: Google Search Console, sc-domain:mattrainc.com, 16 months, 800 distinct queries.
  • Service lines were mapped only to work Mattra actually sells, checked against the site and llms.txt. Heat pumps, gutters, decks, windows, chimneys and stoves all show strong Maine demand and are deliberately excluded, because Mattra does not offer them. Heat pump rebate terms alone are 720 searches a month and are already blocked as negatives.
  • The fortnight figures are interpolated from monthly indices. They are accurate to within a week or so at the turns, which is the right resolution for budget planning and the wrong resolution for claiming a specific day.
  • Not modelled: competitor ad activity, actual close rates by service, crew capacity, and the revenue per job that would turn these demand indices into a profit ranking. A high-volume cheap-click line is not automatically the most profitable one.
