Program Update · Effective October 1, 2026

Efficiency Maine Insulation Rebates Are Changing on October 1

The rebate stops being a percentage of what you spend and becomes a flat amount for each area of your home you insulate. For most Maine homeowners doing more than one area, that means more money back — and a number you can know before the work starts.

The short version

  • Old way: your rebate was a percentage of your project cost, capped by income tier.
  • New way: your rebate is a flat dollar amount for each area you insulate — attic, wall, and basement each count separately.
  • Air sealing now earns its own rebate, separate from insulation.
  • Lifetime cap: up to $8,600 per home, or $5,600 per mobile home.
  • Applies to work completed on or after October 1, 2026. Projects finished before then follow the current rules.

What actually changed

Through September 30, 2026

A share of what you spend

Your rebate was calculated as a percentage of your total project cost, with a dollar ceiling that depended on your income tier. Spend more, get more — until you hit the cap. You could not know your exact rebate until the project was priced.

Starting October 1, 2026

A set amount per area

Each area of your home you insulate earns a fixed rebate, set by how much space is insulated there and your income tier. Cost no longer drives the rebate — so you can know your number before anyone picks up a tool.

Why this is good news for most homeowners. Because every area counts on its own, a whole-home project now earns considerably more than a single-room fix. A homeowner insulating their attic, walls, and basement collects three separate rebates instead of one capped percentage — and it is far easier to understand what you are getting.

What counts as an “area”

Efficiency Maine calls these zones. There are three, and your home can earn one insulation rebate and one air sealing rebate in each.

Attic

All attics and cathedral ceilings combined, including the floors, walls, and slopes exposed to any attic area.

Wall

All exterior framed walls of the home combined. Mobile home walls are not included.

Basement or Underbelly

Floors separating heated space from unheated space or outside air, and concrete foundation walls — or, for a mobile home not on a foundation, the underbelly.

Lifetime limits. One insulation rebate and one air sealing rebate per area, per building or condominium. Total rebates are capped at $8,600 per home, or $5,600 per mobile home.

Who qualifies for which tier

TierHow you qualify
Low incomeSomeone in the household participates in HEAP, SNAP, TANF, or MaineCare. Verification required.
Moderate incomeAdjusted Gross Income up to $70,000 for individual filers, or $100,000 for joint filers. Verification required.
Any incomeNo income requirement. Every eligible Maine homeowner qualifies at this level.

Other program requirements still apply: the home must be in Maine, the insulated space must be heated all heating season, the work cannot be part of new construction or an addition, and the work must be installed by an Efficiency Maine Residential Registered Vendor. Low and moderate income tiers also require an owner-occupied principal residence. Rebate claim forms must be emailed or postmarked within six months of project completion.

What this means for your project

The date that matters is when the work is completed, not when you request a quote or sign a contract.

Finishing before October 1, 2026? Your project falls under the current program — a percentage of project cost, capped by income tier. Nothing about your rebate changes.

Finishing on or after October 1, 2026? Your project falls under the new per-area rebates described on this page. If you are starting a conversation now, this is almost certainly the program that applies to you.
