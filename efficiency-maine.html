Program Overview

Efficiency Maine Insulation Rebate Program

The Efficiency Maine Home Rebate Program helps Maine homeowners offset the cost of home insulation improvements. Through the Home Energy Savings Program (HESP), homeowners at any income level can receive rebates that cover a significant percentage of their insulation project.

The program works through a network of Registered Vendors like Mattra. We perform an energy assessment, identify where your home is losing heat, and recommend the scope of work that qualifies for the highest rebate. After the installation, we submit the documentation and you receive the rebate.

For most Maine homes, insulation and air sealing are the two upgrades with the fastest return on investment. The rebate reduces the upfront cost. The energy savings reduce your heating bills every year after that.

Any-Income Rebates Available

Eligibility

Who Qualifies for Efficiency Maine Insulation Rebates?

The rebate program is available to most Maine homeowners. Below are the requirements and exclusions.

You Qualify If:

  • Your building is in Maine
  • It is a single-family home, two-unit duplex, condominium, mobile home, or mixed-use building with one or two housing units and no commercial electric meter
  • The space being insulated is heated for the entire heating season
  • The work is done by an Efficiency Maine Residential Registered Vendor
  • You file the claim within six months of finishing the work
  • You have not already reached the lifetime limit for the building

Does Not Qualify:

  • New construction or additions
  • Buildings with three or more housing units
  • Buildings with a commercial electric meter
  • Mobile home walls and skirting (the underbelly does qualify)
  • Space that already has more insulation than the program limit

Rentals and Seasonal Homes

Rental properties and seasonal homes qualify at the any-income tier, as long as the space being insulated is heated for the entire heating season. The low- and moderate-income tiers also require the home to be the owner's principal residence, and the household must be verified by Efficiency Maine before the work starts.

Existing Insulation Limits

The rebate only covers space whose existing insulation is at or below a limit: attics R-21 or less, walls R-5 or less, basements R-5 or less, and mobile home underbellies R-11 or less. Any portion above the limit is excluded. Efficiency Maine also requires blower door tests before and after the work (with a few exceptions), and the finished job must meet its Weatherization Requirements Checklist, including R-49 for a blown-in attic. Mattra measures what you have at the free assessment, so you know before any work starts whether an area qualifies.

Any-Income Tier

Maine Insulation Rebates at Every Income Level

You do not need to be low-income to benefit from the Efficiency Maine rebate program. The any-income tier is available to every qualifying building in Maine, including rentals and seasonal homes, with no income verification required.

What the Any-Income Tier Covers

  • Air Sealing — its own rebate of up to $300 (attic), $200 (basement or mobile home underbelly), or $100 (living space). Sealing air leaks around framing, plumbing, and electrical penetrations is the foundation of any insulation project.
  • Insulation — up to $2,000 per area at any income ($1,000 for a smaller area). Covers attic, wall, and basement insulation upgrades including blown-in insulation, spray foam, and dense-pack cellulose.

Lifetime Limits

The program pays one insulation rebate and one air sealing rebate per area, up to a lifetime total of $8,600 per building ($5,600 for a mobile home). Rebates you already received count toward that total, including any paid under the earlier percentage program, so a $4,000 rebate in the past leaves $4,600. A mobile home qualifies for a basement or an underbelly rebate, but not both, and mobile home walls and skirting are not covered. To finance what is left after the rebate, see Efficiency Maine Home Energy Loans.

Covered Upgrades

Insulation Upgrades That Qualify for Maine Rebates

The Efficiency Maine program covers multiple types of insulation work. Each addresses a different part of your home's thermal envelope.

Air Sealing

Sealing air leaks around plumbing, wiring, ductwork, and framing penetrations stops the heat loss that insulation alone cannot address. Air sealing now earns its own rebate, so it does not have to be paired with insulation.

Learn About Air Sealing

Attic Insulation

The largest single source of heat loss in most Maine homes. Blown-in cellulose or fiberglass brings attic R-values up to current standards, reducing heating costs and preventing ice dams.

Learn About Attic Insulation

Wall Insulation

Older Maine homes often have minimal or compressed wall insulation. Dense-pack cellulose and blown-in insulation methods upgrade thermal performance for residential insulation contractors projects without major renovation.

Learn About Wall Insulation

Basement Insulation

Cold basement walls and rim joists let heat escape from the entire house above. Spray foam and rigid board insulation seal the basement envelope and improve comfort on every floor.
