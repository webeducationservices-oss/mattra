Why basements and crawl spaces get mold

A damp basement can have more than one source: seepage through the foundation walls, leaks, poor drainage, or humidity that never fully goes away. In many homes the moisture problem is bigger than it first appears.

Crawl spaces are especially prone to it. In Maine’s climate, a vented crawl space takes in humid summer air that hits cold surfaces and condenses, which creates exactly the conditions mold needs. An unencapsulated crawl space is one of the biggest sources of moisture, mold, and energy loss in a Maine home.

  • A musty smell in the basement or rising into the house.
  • Damp corners, or a basement that never feels fully dry.
  • Visible growth on joists, walls, or stored belongings.
  • Condensation, or air that feels sticky downstairs.

Common questions

What causes mold in a basement or crawl space?

Moisture that keeps getting in: seepage through the foundation, leaks, poor drainage, or humidity that never fully goes away. In a vented crawl space, humid summer air condensing on cold surfaces is a common cause in Maine.

Will removing the mold be enough?

Not if the moisture is still getting in. That is why we find the source and fix it with encapsulation, waterproofing, or dehumidification, depending on what the source is.

What is crawl space encapsulation?

It turns an open, vented crawl space into a sealed, dry space. A heavy-duty vapor barrier goes across the ground and up the foundation walls, the walls are insulated, and a dehumidifier keeps the air dry.

How long does mold removal take?

Most residential remediations take 1 to 5 days depending on scope. Moisture control work can add time, and you get a realistic timeline after the first assessment.
