Common Issues

How Poor Siding, Flashing, and Exterior Details Lead to Moisture and Air Intrusion

Siding does not fail in isolation. When exterior details are not working correctly, outside air and water can move behind the cladding and begin affecting the structure, insulation, and indoor conditions.

Siding and Flashing Trouble Spots

Weak points around rooflines, trim, and transitions can let moisture move behind the exterior barrier.

Window and Door Intrusion

Openings are common places for water and air to enter when details are not sealed correctly.

Gaps and Failed Joints

Cracks and separation can allow outside air and water into wall systems.

Improper Installation Details

Even newer siding can underperform if the assembly details were not handled correctly.

Hidden Moisture Damage

Problems behind siding often stay hidden until rot, mold, or staining starts to show.

Comfort and Efficiency Loss

Air leaks through exterior walls can make rooms feel drafty and harder to heat or cool.

These problems matter because moisture intrusion behind siding can lead to hidden damage before the homeowner sees obvious signs. Over time, siding problems that cause rot, mold, or insulation damage can become much more expensive than the original exterior issue.

Rot Around Openings

When siding or flashing allows moisture to reach wood framing, rot can develop around windows, doors, and trim. This is one of the most common connections between siding failure and structural damage.

Mold and Musty Odors

Trapped moisture behind siding can contribute to hidden mold growth and indoor air concerns.

Mold Remediation →

Mold and Musty Odors

When moisture gets behind the exterior barrier and stays trapped, it creates conditions for mold growth inside wall cavities. Homeowners may notice musty smells or air quality issues before seeing visible damage.

Air Intrusion

Exterior wall leakage can make rooms feel drafty and reduce overall comfort.

Air Sealing →

Air Intrusion and Energy Loss

Gaps in siding, failed caulking, and improper flashing allow outside air into wall cavities. This makes rooms drafty, harder to heat, and reduces the performance of wall insulation.
